\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly explored for cyber-physical systems (CPS), including industrial control and automation settings. However, most existing efforts either analyze static datasets, use LLMs only for offline assistance, or evaluate model behavior without a realistic closed-loop execution environment. This limitation makes it difficult to study whether LLM-driven attack and defense strategies remain effective when exposed to real controller timing, process dynamics, and safety constraints.

This paper presents \textbf{CPSForge}, a closed-loop LLM red-team/blue-team testbed for cyber-physical systems. CPSForge is built around a real hardware-in-the-loop environment consisting of a Siemens PLC programmed in TIA Portal v17 and connected to Factory I/O as the physical-process emulator. Within this environment, an LLM-based attacker proposes structured process-aware attack actions, a runtime safety shield mediates every experiment, and a defender stack detects anomalous or unsafe behavior. Failed or weakly detected attacks are stored as hard cases to support iterative defender refinement across rounds.

The goal of CPSForge is not to allow unrestricted LLM control of industrial processes, but to provide a safe, auditable, and reproducible framework for evaluating how LLMs can generate CPS-grounded attack strategies and how defenders can improve under repeated adversarial pressure. The system supports multiple process scenes, reproducible logging, replay-based analysis, and rigorous measurement of attack success, stealth, physical impact, detection latency, and adaptation gains. A preliminary evaluation across three Factory I/O scenes demonstrates that the framework produces measurable process impact from structured attacks, achieves perfect detector recall with threshold and invariant detectors, and generates reproducible experiment artifacts suitable for iterative analysis.
\end{abstract}
