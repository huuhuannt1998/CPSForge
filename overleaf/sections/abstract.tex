\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly studied for cyber-physical systems (CPS) security, but most prior work relies on static datasets, offline analysis, or simulated evaluations without realistic closed-loop execution. This limits our understanding of whether LLM-driven attack and defense strategies remain effective under real controller timing, process dynamics, and safety constraints.

This paper presents \textbf{CPSForge}, a closed-loop LLM red-team/blue-team testbed for CPS security experimentation. CPSForge connects a real Siemens S7 PLC programmed in TIA Portal~v17 to Factory~I/O, a software-based industrial process emulator, forming a PLC-in-the-loop evaluation environment with authentic controller scan timing and I/O behaviour. It supports both \emph{batch mode}, where scripted, random, or LLM-based attackers propose structured attack actions through a mediated pipeline, and \emph{live agent mode}, where autonomous LLM attacker and defender agents interact concurrently under a coordinator that enforces runtime safety rules on all PLC writes. All actions are logged for reproducible analysis.

We evaluate CPSForge across five Factory~I/O scenes using five attacker variants and six anomaly detectors. Across 96~experiment runs (68~eval-flagged) totalling 11{,}864~time-series steps and 207~executed attack actions, the structured action schema reliably translated both manual and LLM-generated attacks into concrete PLC writes, while the safety shield rejected out-of-policy proposals in constrained scenes. A four-phase campaign attacker exposed a coverage gap in rule-based detectors on Level Control (F1~=~0.00, 102~false negatives); after a single Isolation Forest retraining cycle on the hard-case trace, detector F1 recovered to 0.92 with recall rising from 0.00 to 1.00 on the same scene. In live agent mode, a 7B parameter model achieved 95--98\% shield approval across four of five scenes. To our knowledge, CPSForge is the first open framework to integrate LLM-driven attack generation, an LLM defender agent, runtime safety enforcement, and closed-loop detector adaptation, all executing against a real PLC.
\end{abstract}
