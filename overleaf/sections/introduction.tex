\section{Introduction}

Cyber-physical systems (CPS) tightly integrate computation, communication, and physical processes. Industrial control systems, smart manufacturing environments, and automated production lines increasingly depend on programmable logic controllers (PLCs), sensors, actuators, and supervisory software to maintain correct and safe operation. At the same time, recent progress in large language models (LLMs) has introduced new possibilities for automation, reasoning, and system interaction. These advances raise an important question: can LLMs be used in a principled way to study both offensive and defensive CPS behavior under realistic process constraints?

Existing LLM-for-CPS research remains limited in several important ways. First, many studies are performed on static datasets or synthetic traces, which do not fully capture the timing behavior and control feedback of a live control loop. Second, LLMs are often evaluated as advisory tools rather than as components embedded in a closed-loop system with concrete actuation consequences. Third, the literature rarely studies attacker and defender evolution together in one reproducible framework. As a result, it is still unclear how effective LLM-generated CPS attacks are when grounded in real process context, how safely such attacks can be evaluated, and whether defenders can systematically improve from attack-induced failures.

This paper proposes \textbf{CPSForge}, a closed-loop LLM red-team/blue-team CPS testbed designed to address these gaps. CPSForge is built around a real Siemens PLC connected to Factory I/O, with control logic engineered in TIA Portal v17. The framework enables an LLM attacker to reason over scene context, process variables, and attacker objectives, then propose structured attack actions such as sensor spoofing, actuator overrides, setpoint shifts, timing perturbations, or sequence disruptions. Importantly, all candidate actions are mediated by a runtime safety shield before any write reaches the live PLC. This design ensures that experimentation remains bounded, auditable, and aligned with process-specific safety constraints.

On the defensive side, CPSForge supports threshold-based, invariant-based, and learned detectors. The core closed-loop idea is that unsuccessful detections are not treated as isolated failures. Instead, missed or weakly detected attacks are collected into a hard-case bank and used to refine the defender over repeated rounds. This design enables the testbed to evaluate not only attack quality and shield effectiveness, but also how defender robustness evolves under sustained adversarial pressure.

The central thesis of this work is that a realistic hardware-in-the-loop CPS environment, combined with structured LLM attack planning, runtime safety enforcement, and adaptive defense, provides a stronger basis for studying LLM use in CPS security than offline-only or one-shot evaluations. Rather than claiming that LLMs should directly control industrial systems, the paper advocates for a carefully mediated framework that exposes both the promise and the risks of LLM-enabled CPS reasoning.

The contributions of this paper are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item We present CPSForge, a closed-loop LLM red-team/blue-team testbed for cyber-physical systems built around a real Siemens PLC and Factory I/O hardware-in-the-loop environment.
    \item We introduce a structured CPS attack framework in which an LLM proposes process-aware attack actions while a runtime safety shield mediates all live writes.
    \item We design a defender workflow that supports hard-case collection and iterative adaptation across repeated attack rounds.
    \item We provide a preliminary evaluation across three Factory I/O scenes using scripted and random attackers, demonstrating measurable attack impact, safety shield mediation, and defender detection under live PLC execution.
\end{itemize}
