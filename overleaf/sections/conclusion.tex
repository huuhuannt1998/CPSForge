\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented CPSForge, a closed-loop LLM red-team/blue-team testbed for cyber-physical systems built around a real Siemens S7 PLC, TIA Portal~v17, and Factory~I/O as a software-based process emulator. The design emphasises PLC-in-the-loop execution with authentic controller timing, structured process-aware attack generation, mandatory runtime safety mediation, and defender adaptation from hard cases collected across repeated rounds. A key contribution is the \emph{live agent mode}, in which an autonomous LLM attacker and an LLM defender operate concurrently on the same physical process, coordinated through a safety-enforcing mediator.

The evaluation across five Factory~I/O scenes and five attacker variants---96~runs from 54~experiment configurations, comprising 68~eval-flagged runs totalling 11{,}864~time-series steps---yields several findings. The structured action schema reliably translates both manual and LLM-generated attacks into concrete PLC writes. Level Control is the most exploitable scene (scripted ASR~=~0.75, LLM batch ASR~=~0.33), while Sorting by Height shows the greatest resilience (ASR $\leq$ 0.12). The LLM batch attacker achieved the highest ASR (0.67) on Sorting by Weight by targeting threshold parameters, though it underperformed random baselines on other scenes---suggesting that LLM attack value is scene-dependent and warrants further controlled comparison. The campaign attacker exposed a coverage gap in rule-based detectors on Level Control (stealth~=~1.0, 102~false negatives), demonstrating a known limitation of fixed-threshold monitors when facing gradually-escalating perturbations. After a single Isolation Forest retraining cycle on these 102~hard-case steps, detector F1 recovered from 0.00 to 0.92 with perfect recall; however, this recovery reflects learning a specific attack pattern rather than demonstrated generalisation to novel campaigns. The safety shield approved all submitted actions in most batch configurations, while rejecting out-of-policy proposals in sorting scenes and From A to B (20--30\% rejected) and strict-invariant violations in Level Control agent mode (21/25 rejected), confirming its role as a reliable safety boundary.

Agent-mode experiments with Qwen2-7B-Instruct (7B parameters) achieved 95--98\% shield approval rates across four of five scenes, with zero attacker errors, while the 3.8B Phi-3 model achieved only 2\% schema compliance---establishing a practical minimum model capacity for structured CPS agent operation. Level Control remains the exception (12\% approval) due to strict process invariants.

A cross-detector comparison across six approaches reveals that one-class ML detectors trained on a short 300-step baseline trace achieve the highest F1 scores (OCSVM: 0.62--0.66; Isolation Forest: 0.55--0.62), establishing relative ordering among detector types even with minimal training data. The diversity of precision--recall and latency trade-offs motivates ensemble approaches for future CPS defence.

Several limitations constrain the current findings: all results use a single LLM family (Qwen2-7B), a single PLC model, and software-emulated rather than physical plant processes; many (scene, attacker) pairs have only one or two runs, yielding wide confidence intervals; and the adaptation mechanism has been demonstrated on only one scene with one retraining cycle. These limitations define clear directions for future work.

To our knowledge, CPSForge is the first open framework to integrate LLM-driven attack generation, an LLM defender agent, runtime safety enforcement, and closed-loop detector adaptation, all executing against a real PLC. The framework, configurations, and experiment artifacts are released for reproducibility and extension.
