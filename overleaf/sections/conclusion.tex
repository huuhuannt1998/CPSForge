\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented CPSForge, a closed-loop LLM red-team/blue-team testbed for cyber-physical systems built around a real Siemens PLC, TIA Portal v17, and Factory I/O. The design emphasizes realistic hardware-in-the-loop execution, structured process-aware attack generation, mandatory runtime safety mediation, and defender adaptation from hard cases collected across repeated rounds.

The main motivation behind CPSForge is that LLMs should not be studied in CPS security only through static datasets or unconstrained prompting. Instead, their utility and risk should be evaluated in a realistic, auditable, and safety-bounded environment that captures controller timing, process feedback, and detector response. CPSForge provides such an environment.

A preliminary evaluation across three Factory I/O scenes using scripted and random attackers demonstrates that the framework produces measurable and differentiated results. Level Control attacks achieved 100\% success with a process impact of 1.450, while From A to B attacks produced moderate impact (ASR = 0.67 scripted, 0.30 random), and Sorting by Height resisted all single-shot attacks. Threshold and invariant detectors achieved perfect recall across all configurations with F1 scores ranging from 0.680 to 0.821. The safety shield approved all valid actions while filtering unsafe random perturbations. All experiment artifacts are structured, reproducible, and suitable for iterative analysis.

Future work will evaluate the LLM attacker module, extend to additional scenes, improve detector precision through learned models and adaptive thresholds, and exercise the closed-loop adaptation mechanism with hard cases generated by more subtle attack strategies. More broadly, CPSForge provides a foundation for safe LLM integration in cyber-physical environments, including multi-scene experiments, richer attack campaigns, and explicit study of LLM-specific risks such as prompt injection and unsafe actuation.
