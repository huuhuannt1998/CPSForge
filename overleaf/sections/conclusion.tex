\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented CPSForge, a closed-loop LLM red-team/blue-team testbed for cyber-physical systems built around a real Siemens S7 PLC, TIA Portal~v17, and Factory~I/O. The design emphasises realistic hardware-in-the-loop execution, structured process-aware attack generation, mandatory runtime safety mediation, and defender adaptation from hard cases collected across repeated rounds. A key contribution is the \emph{live agent mode}, in which an autonomous LLM attacker and an LLM defender operate concurrently on the same physical process, coordinated through a safety-enforcing mediator.

The evaluation across three Factory~I/O scenes and four attacker variants---totalling 55~runs and 4{,}465~live PLC steps---demonstrates several findings. All attacker variants achieved 100\% action validity and execution success in batch mode, confirming that the structured action schema reliably translates both manual and LLM-generated attacks into concrete PLC writes. Scripted attacks achieved the highest per-attack success rate (ASR~=~1.0 on Level Control), while the LLM batch attacker generated fewer but targeted actions with comparable impact. The safety shield approved all well-formed actions and blocked only random out-of-range perturbations (20\% on Sorting), confirming its role as a reliable hard safety boundary. Threshold and invariant detectors achieved perfect recall (1.0) across all batch-mode configurations, with precision ranging from 0.01 to 0.70 depending on the ratio of attack steps to total steps.

Agent-mode experiments demonstrated the full multi-agent architecture operating against the live PLC, but revealed that small local models (Phi-3-Mini-4K-Instruct, 3.8B parameters) achieve only 1.6--2.1\% schema compliance for structured CPS attack actions---establishing a practical lower bound on model capacity for effective CPS agent operation. Combined with the 7B-model batch results (100\% validity), this brackets the minimum model size needed for reliable structured CPS reasoning.

To our knowledge, CPSForge is the first framework to combine LLM-driven attack generation, an autonomous LLM defender, a configurable runtime safety shield, closed-loop adaptation, and live multi-agent operation, all executing against a real PLC in a hardware-in-the-loop setting. The framework, configurations, and experiment artifacts are designed for reproducibility and extensibility, providing a foundation for continued study of LLM-enabled CPS security.
